# 12 — UI: pictures first, words on demand

Player feedback: the ledger screens read as walls of text. The rules every screen follows now:

1. **Picture, then number, then sentence.** An icon and a value carry the state; the sentence that explains it lives in a tooltip (hover, keyboard focus, or a long press on touch). `tip()` in `src/ui/kit.ts`.
2. **Progressive disclosure.** Show the one line that matters; fold the rest (`fold()`, a `<details>` that remembers whether you left it open). Pending decisions (perk offers, a radio deadline) stay open and prominent; history (chronicle, full logs, backstory) starts closed.
3. **Font hierarchy.** Five steps only: `.t-xl` screen title, `.t-l` section head, `.t-m` body, `.t-s` secondary, `.t-xs` small-caps meta. Big numbers for what you act on (mag, wall, mood, hours).
4. **Glanceable rows.** Status as chips (`chip()`), skills as pips (`pips()`), relationships as a heart/crack icon and a number.
5. **The HUD speaks only when it matters.** Contextual key hints (reload when empty, the nest when near it), one message line at a time, the full key list on demand.
6. **Keep the voice.** The lore stays in the tooltips, folds and the chronicle; the surface is short.

References: RimWorld's inspect pane and hover-to-explain mood numbers; Darkest Dungeon's icon-led hero sheet with tooltips; Frostpunk's icon-first resource bar; Slay the Spire's keyword hover cards; Nielsen Norman Group on progressive disclosure.
